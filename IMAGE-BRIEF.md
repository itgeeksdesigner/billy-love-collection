## 3. Image direction for the Warm Nude palette

**Core rule: the garment is the only saturated thing in any frame.**

**Use:** warm plaster and limewash walls, sand and oat seamless, pale concrete, raw linen, light oak, terracotta-cream, sunlit interiors. Tones between `#FBF7F2` and `#C4A88E`.

**Avoid:** cool grey or white studio, blue daylight, foliage-heavy green scenes, neon, black backdrops, glossy or tech surfaces.

**Lighting:** soft, diffused, warm. Golden-hour window light or a large diffused key. Low contrast, gentle shadows, no hard flash, no true black — dark sections are espresso `#2B2320`, not black, so imagery that goes fully black reads as a hole in the page.

| Section | Image type |
|---|---|
| Hero | Lifestyle, three-quarter body, warm interior, model right, left 45% clear |
| Product cards | Flat warm studio, consistent crop and distance across every card |
| Promo banners | Wide lifestyle, model to one side, clean space for the copy |
| Feature / Details | Macro fabric and trim, calm centre where the white card sits |
| Product of the week | Single hero shot, generous negative space |
| Journal | Warm lifestyle or tactile detail, landscape 7:5 |
| Social feed | Deliberately rougher — real phone photos, natural light, imperfect framing |

**Consistency:** pick one skin-tone and white-balance treatment and apply it to every image. On a palette this quiet, a 200K white-balance drift between adjacent cards reads as a mistake.

**Accent discipline:** the red on the camo tee and the pink on the tracksuit are the only strong colours on the page. Let photography carry them — don't add red or pink to buttons or headings. Clay `#C4A88E` stays the UI accent.
